Conventions. [V] = checked this session against a fetched/searched source (abstract-level only; full texts NOT read). [R] = recalled from training knowledge, not re-verified now: verify volume/page before citing in a package README. "Spine" = predication-chu-spine.md. Where the spine overclaims I say CORRECTION.

## 0. Headline corrections to the spine (read first)

1. **Task-brief citation error.** "Fernandes et al. 2021 Nature on hydrodynamics/vortices" is two different papers. Nature 595:537-541 (2021) is **Falcucci et al.**, "Extreme flow simulations reveal skeletal adaptations of deep-sea sponges" [V]. Fernandes et al. 2021 is **J. R. Soc. Interface 18:20210559**, "Mechanical and hydrodynamic analyses of helical strake-like ridges in a glass sponge" [V]. Fernandes 2020 is Nature Materials 20:237 "Mechanically robust lattices inspired by deep-sea glass sponges" (diagonal bracing, best buckling resistance per material) [V-partial]. The Falcucci feeding/reproduction inference is under a published Matters Arising (DTU) arguing the model ignores the sponge tissue, a near-impermeable syncytial sheet [V]. Do not cite "vortex-assisted feeding" as settled.
2. **"Lambek sound for the syntactic concept lattice" is true but trivial; *-autonomy is NOT inherited.** Any residuated lattice is a sound model of (Full) Lambek calculus. The spine's "cyclic *-autonomous via Chu on the concatenation quantale" needs a cyclic dualizing element; the language-induced polarity (l,r) vs w is NOT cyclic in general (L need not be closed under rotation), and the concept lattice B(Σ*, Σ*×Σ*, ⊢) has no involutive negation. What is true: Chu(Set,K) is *-autonomous (Barr); the 2-valued extensional collapse gives the concept lattice; the lattice carries residuals (/, \) but not an involution. The syntactic concept lattice lives in the non-involutive part (Lambek / FL), the Chu space around it in the *-autonomous part. State the Chu-Lambek link as an adjunction/embedding to be proved, not an identity.
3. **"k > 1 = criticality" is mis-stated.** In branching-process/critical-neural-network literature, criticality is branching ratio sigma = 1; sigma > 1 is supercritical (runaway). In autocatalytic networks (Jain-Krishna 1998) the transition is at largest eigenvalue lambda_1 = 1 of the catalytic adjacency. In Hall's EROI literature, EROI = 1 is merely break-even; society needs about 3 or much more. Recommend splitting: k<1 starved, k=1 critical, k>1 growth, k >= k_min (a measured floor, cf. Hall) viable.
4. **"Every identification costs a Landauer bit"** is a bookkeeping convention, not a consequence of Landauer. Landauer bounds *logically irreversible erasure* (many-to-one on physical states). Merging two bubbles whose provenance is kept in the content-addressed history is logically reversible (Bennett 1973) and costs nothing at the bound; the kT ln2 is paid only when the pre-merge distinction is discarded (garbage collection, compaction, forgetting). The ledger is honest as "bits of distinction retired", priced at kT ln2 as an unreachable floor (real hardware is ~10^3 to 10^6 above it). That is still a useful, falsifiable metric. Flag as definition, not law.
5. **Dedekind-MacNeille != GKP causal boundary.** The DM completion of a causal poset is B(P,P,<=) (all pairs (A,B) with B = upper bounds of A, A = lower bounds of B). The GKP boundary uses only *indecomposable* past sets (IP/IF; TIP/TIF) and then an identification relation (Szabados / Marolf-Ross / Flores-Herrera-Sanchez). Harris proved a universality statement relating them in the strongly causal Lorentzian case. For a **finite** causal set every IP is principal, so the TIP/TIF boundary is empty; only the unbounded/limit (infinite growth) case is non-vacuous. So "GKP lives here" holds for the infinite limit of a growing corpus, not for any finite SignCell store.
6. **Causally-closed sets: ortholattice always, orthomodular only with geometry.** For any symmetric irreflexive orthogonality, the polarity S -> S-perp gives a complete ortholattice (S meet S-perp = empty, S join S-perp = whole). Orthomodularity (a<=b => b = a v (b ^ a-perp)) is an extra theorem: Cegla-Jadczyk for Minkowski, Casini 2002 for general Lorentzian [V]; for discrete causal nets it can fail unless a K-density condition holds [V via Casini-related summary]. So in a corpus, *test* orthomodularity; do not assume it. This is the cleanest falsifiable invariant the Lean/mjs layer can state.
7. **Bead-weave parse = only the elimination fragment.** A purely local rule "A/B next to B yields A" generates exactly AB grammars (Bar-Hillel-Gaifman-Shamir 1960: context-free). Lambek's introduction rules (hypothetical reasoning: type-raising, composition) need non-local hypotheses; chart parsers for L (Hepple; Konig; Fowler 2008; Moot-Retore) are more than a plain CYK triangle. Full L is still context-free in weak generative power (Pentus) and recognition is NP-complete (Pentus 2006, product-free L) and polynomial for bounded order (Pentus 2010). Keep the weave picture, but the "Wang rule" is the elimination rule only; introduction = a seam where a sub-weave is closed off.
8. **Learnability from positive data is only for sub-classes.** The full syntactic concept lattice of a context-free language is infinite and CFLs are not identifiable in the limit from positive data (Gold 1967). Clark's results are for restricted classes (substitutable, k,l-substitutable, DLG, CBFG) and for MAT-style teachers; "learnable from positive data (+ membership queries)" must name the class. See sec 1.3.

## 1. Language side

### 1.1 Clark: syntactic concept lattice
- A. Clark, "A learnable representation for syntax using residuated lattices", Formal Grammar 2009, LNCS 5591; PDF https://members.loria.fr/PdeGroote/FG09/Clark.pdf [V]. Statement: Galois connection between contexts (l,r) and strings w (w in context iff l w r in L) gives a lattice of concepts (the "syntactic concept lattice", SCL). Product of two concepts is the closure of the concatenation of their extents; with residuals this makes the lattice a residuated (monoidal) lattice. Abstract: a "richly structured context-sensitive formalism that can be learned using a generalisation of distributional learning"; discusses relation to categorial grammar and CFG. Also Clark, "Learning context free grammars with the syntactic concept lattice", ICGI 2010 (Royal Holloway record) [V-title].
- Plug: the exact source of the spine sec 1. Row = word, column = context, matrix = L-incidence over 2. FOUR extension: l w r attested / refuted.
- Caveat: Clark's lattice product is the closed concatenation; the sound-for-Lambek claim is just "residuated lattice". Infinite for non-regular L; finite iff L regular (Myhill-Nerode).

DLGs: lattice-based, represent all regular languages, some but not all CFLs, and some non-CFLs; a simple learning algorithm is given. ### 1.3 Wurm: completeness of SCL
- C. Wurm, "Completeness of full Lambek calculus for syntactic concept lattices", Formal Grammar 2012 (17th conf.), LNCS 7395, from p.126 [V; Wurm's page titles it "Completeness of Syntactic Concept Lattices for the Full Lambek Calculus"; some catalogs date it FG 2013]. Statement: SCLs, as a subclass of residuated lattices, form a *complete* class of models for the Full Lambek calculus FL (with additive conjunction/disjunction, 0/1): FL-derivable iff valid in every SCL (and, by Wurm, the lattice-ordered extension). So the logic of SCLs is exactly FL.
- C. Wurm, "Language-theoretic and finite relation models for the (full) Lambek calculus", J. Logic Lang. Inf. 26 (2017) 179-214 [R], and Springer chapter https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-662-53826-5_19 "extends established completeness results for FL-bot and its fragments for SCL to regular languages and finite algebras" [V]. Kuznetsov's 2025 arXiv:2510.24853 [V] credits Wurm (2017) with the modified semantics "models over syntactic concept lattices" because plain L-models lose completeness once union, intersection, Kleene iteration or constants (empty set, {eps}) are added; Kuznetsov extends the SCL semantics to infinitary action logic (strong completeness; two-letter alphabet).
- Pentus: M. Pentus, "Lambek grammars are context free", LICS 1993 (Moscow tech report 1992); Pentus, "Language completeness of the Lambek calculus", LICS 1994 (derivable iff true in all L-models, i.e. language-theoretic models); Pentus, "Models for the Lambek calculus", Ann. Pure Appl. Logic 75 (1995) 179-213; Pentus, "Lambek calculus is NP-complete", TCS 357 (2006) 186-201 (product-free); Pentus, "A polynomial-time algorithm for Lambek grammars of bounded order", Linguistic Analysis 36 (2010) [R]. Buszkowski, "Completeness results for Lambek syntactic calculus", Z. Math. Logik 32 (1986) [R]. The L-model completeness statement for Lambek is [V via Kuznetsov: Pentus [36,37]; CF result [35]].
- Plug: this is the closing edge `FCALambekCircle.lean` leaves as documented-boundary. It is a theorem about *derivability vs validity in all SCLs*, i.e. the logic, not about any one lattice. For the executable closing edge you need: (a) a finite SCL computed from a finite sample (regular language approximation, Wurm's finite-algebra case), (b) a checker that a Lambek sequent holds in that SCL (decidable), (c) the converse direction is Wurm's completeness, which you'd quote, not re-prove.
- CAUTION: Wurm's completeness is over *all* SCLs of *all* languages; it does not say "the SCL of English is complete for Lambek".
- Related prior art not in spine: Lambek pregroups (Lambek 1999, 2008) and DisCoCat (Coecke, Sadrzadeh, Clark 2010, "Mathematical foundations for a compositional distributional model of meaning", Linguistic Analysis 36) use *compact closed* categories (a special *-autonomous) with vector semantics; Bradley, Terilla, Vlassopoulos, "An enriched category theory of language: from syntax to semantics", La Matematica 2022 (arXiv 2106.07890) [R] build a [0,1]-enriched category from text statistics whose Isbell-type completion is a V-valued analogue of the concept lattice. These are the nearest relatives of "Chu space of language"; position the new work against them.

## 2. Chu / channel theory

- M. Barr, *-Autonomous Categories, LNM 752, Springer 1979, with appendix by Po-Hsiang Chu. Statement: for a symmetric monoidal closed category V with pullbacks and an object K, Chu(V,K) is *-autonomous with dualizing object K = (1, K, hom); for V = Set: objects (A, r, X) with r: A x X -> K, morphisms (f,g) with r'(f a, x') = r(a, g x'); tensor (A,X)(x)(B,Y) = (A x B, Hom(A,Y) x Hom(B,X)); dual swaps points and states (and transposes r). Also Barr, "*-autonomous categories and linear logic", Math. Struct. Comp. Sci. 1 (1991) 159-178; Barr, "The Chu construction" (1996, TAC or Documenta Math.; [R] verify venue). Seely 1989, "Linear logic, *-autonomous categories and cofree coalgebras", Contemp. Math. 92 [R] (models of MLL); Lafont-Streicher, "Game semantics for linear logic", LICS 1991 [R].
- Plug: Chu(Set, FOUR) is *-autonomous provided FOUR is merely a SET (the Set-based construction needs no structure on K). Hence the spine's `Chu(Set,FOUR)` claim is fine; FOUR's lattice/bilattice structure is extra and must be put in by hand to get the FOUR tensor/hom of `LinearLogicChu.lean` (i.e., use a K with a monoid / use Chu over the quantale-enriched category). The Chu(Set,K) tensor is not the pointwise lattice product; check which one `LinearLogicChu.lean` implements.
- P. Pratt, "Chu spaces: automata with quantum aspects", PhysComp 94; "Chu spaces and their interpretation as concurrent objects", in Computer Science Today, LNCS 1000 (1995); "The Stone gamut: a coordinatization of mathematics", LICS 1995; "Chu spaces as a semantic bridge between linear logic and mathematics", TCS 294 (2003) 439-471 [R; Chu pages at boole.stanford.edu/chuguide.html]. Statement: Chu(Set,2) embeds Set, Top, Pos, Gph, Grp, Vect-ish via the Stone gamut; a Chu space over 2 is a binary relation (events x states), i.e. an FCA context, and also a "concurrent object" (events/states duality, Hoare-type "ordered by implication"). Biextensional collapse = identify equal rows and columns = FCA clarification.
- Spine reading "clarification/reduction = biextensional collapse" is correct.
- Abramsky, "Coalgebras, Chu spaces, and representations of physical systems", LICS 2010 / J. Philos. Logic 42 (2013) [R]: Chu(Set,2) duality for physical systems; and Abramsky-Brandenburger, "The sheaf-theoretic structure of non-locality and contextuality", New J. Phys. 13 (2011) 113036; Abramsky, Barbosa, Kishida, Lal, Mansfield, "Contextuality, cohomology and paradox", CSL 2015 [R]. These are the prior art for "sheaves over a measurement/site, H^1 obstruction": the cohomological obstruction is *sufficient but not necessary* for contextuality (it detects only some, e.g. logically-contextual/strongly-contextual cases with certain structure). So "H^1 != 0 iff contradictory" is an overclaim; H^1 = 0 does not certify consistency.
- Cellular sheaves with Laplacian: Hansen & Ghrist, "Toward a spectral theory of cellular sheaves", J. Appl. Comput. Topol. 3 (2019) 315-358 [R]: L = delta^T delta; quadratic form x^T L x = total disagreement; ker L = H^0 = global sections (consensus). This is the exact meaning of the spine's "disagreement energy x^T L x"; if the mjs uses ordinary graph Laplacian it's the constant-sheaf special case. Also Curry's thesis (Penn 2014) Sheaves, cosheaves and applications [R]; Bodnar et al., "Neural sheaf diffusion", NeurIPS 2022 [R].
- Barwise & Seligman, *Information Flow: The Logic of Distributed Systems*, Cambridge Tracts in Theoretical Computer Science 44, 1997 [R]. Classification A = (tok(A), typ(A), |=_A). Infomorphism f: A -> B = pair f^: typ(A) -> typ(B), f_: tok(B) -> tok(A) with f_(b) |=_A alpha iff b |=_B f^(alpha). So infomorphisms A->B are Chu morphisms B->A over 2 (direction flips): CORRECTION to spine's wording "infomorphisms = Chu morphisms over 2": exactly, infomorphisms = morphisms of Chu(Set,2)^op. Channels = cospans of infomorphisms into a core; "sum" of classifications = Chu tensor/sum; local logics (Seligman 1991 "Physical situations and information flow") add a theory + normal tokens: a theory-carrying Chu space. Barwise-Seligman's *constraints with exceptions* (non-normal tokens) is an existing formal home for the contested/FOUR cells: a local logic is sound on normal tokens only. Cite when explaining the against-lane.

## 3. Tiles

- J. Kari, "A small aperiodic set of Wang tiles", Discrete Math. 160 (1996) 259-264 (14 tiles); K. Culik II, "An aperiodic set of 13 Wang tiles", Discrete Math. 160 (1996) 245-251 (13 tiles); J. Kari, "A small aperiodic set..." uses the construction in [V-secondary: Culik's JUCS 1(10) PDF and Siefken thesis]. Statement (recalled, [R], verify constants): tiles are encoded as Mealy-machine-like transducers that multiply a real number (read as a base-n word) by a rational; the tileset has a valid tiling iff a piecewise-multiplicative map (multiplication by 2 and 2/3 on [1/3,2]) has an infinite orbit with the right constraints; aperiodicity follows because that map has no periodic orbit (arithmetical argument, powers of 2 and 3). [V]: "start from a piecewise multiplicative function with rational multiplicands on a finite interval... when no periodic points the tiles can only tile aperiodically"; current aperiodicity proofs are number-theoretic, no known hierarchical (substitutive) explanation. Also Kari & Siefken (J. Siefken thesis, "A minimal subsystem of the Kari-Culik tilings", ETDS 2017, arXiv 1410.1572 [V]).
- Plug: justifies the spine's "Wang tile = transducer; stacking rows = composing relations; strip tiling = nonemptiness of n-fold composite". True and standard (Kari 1996; also Berger-style Turing simulation). Kari-Culik is literally an *arithmetic* generative system: rows are multiplications. The exotic point: these are aperiodic with **no hierarchical structure** — a different regime than substitutive (JR is substitutive-ish, see Labbe).
- E. Jeandel & M. Rao, "An aperiodic set of 11 Wang tiles", Advances in Combinatorics 2021:1; arXiv 1506.06492 [V]. Statement: 11 tiles, 4 colors; found by computer search; also proved minimal: no aperiodic Wang set with fewer than 11 tiles, none with fewer than 4 colors (exhaustive search + SAT-type reasoning). Structure (recalled [R]): J-R tilings are a coding of a Z^2 rotation on the 2-torus (Labbe, "Markov partitions for toral Z^2-rotations featuring Jeandel-Rao and Ammann Wang tilings", Discrete Comput Geom 2021 (?) and "Substitutive structure of Jeandel-Rao aperiodic tilings", DCG 65 (2021)); Labbe also gave a finite set of 'substitution' with Rauzy-induction-like structure; Jeandel-Rao hulls have (non-)minimal subtleties. Verify before claiming.
- Berger, "The undecidability of the domino problem", Mem. AMS 66 (1966): first aperiodic set (20426, later 104); Robinson 1971 (56 then 6 shapes); Wang 1961. The domino problem is undecidable (Berger); for the strip of finite height it's decidable (finite automaton emptiness) — matches the spine's "n-fold composite nonemptiness".
- Durand, Romashchenko, Shen, "Fixed-point tile sets and their applications", JCSS 78 (2012) 731-764 [R]: self-referential/fixed-point aperiodic tilings (recursion theorem for tilings). Plug: "recursively self-improving" in tile language: the tile set that simulates itself at a larger scale. Mozes 1989 (substitutions → sofic), Hochman 2009 Invent. (effective subshifts as projections of SFT), Aubrun-Sablik 2013: sofic/effective subshifts = projections of 2D SFTs => any computable constraint family can be a tiling rule. This is the general theorem backing "a grammar's chart constraints are tile rules".
- E. Winfree, *Algorithmic self-assembly of DNA*, Caltech PhD 1998; Winfree et al., "Design and self-assembly of two-dimensional DNA crystals", Nature 394 (1998) 539-544; Rothemund & Winfree, "The program-size complexity of self-assembled squares", STOC 2000; Soloveichik & Winfree, "Complexity of self-assembled shapes", SIAM J. Comput. 36 (2007) 1544-1569 (Kolmogorov-type tile complexity of an n x n square: Theta(log n / log log n)); Rothemund, Papadakis, Winfree, "Algorithmic self-assembly of DNA Sierpinski triangles", PLoS Biol 2 (2004); Doty et al. / Meunier-Woods-et al., "The tile assembly model is intrinsically universal", FOCS 2012; Meunier & Woods STOC 2017 (temperature-1 aTAM not intrinsically universal) [R]. aTAM: tiles with glues of integer strength, temperature tau; a tile attaches to a growing assembly iff total glue strength to existing neighbours >= tau; start from a seed. tau = 2 permits *cooperative* binding and is Turing universal; tau = 1 is not (open/partly resolved). 
- Plug and CONTRAST with Wang: Wang tiling is a **global existence** constraint (does a tiling exist); aTAM is a **growth dynamics from a seed** with **thermal** cooperation threshold. For generative language (autoregressive frontier) the aTAM is the correct model; the Wang view is the static grammar-ness check. Cooperation threshold tau = 2 ~ "a type is enabled only when two adjacent beads agree" ~ residuation needing both neighbours (the same A/B + B pattern). Glue strength = binding energy -> connects tile to the energy/EROEI ledger: Winfree's kTAM (kinetic TAM) gives rates r_f = k e^{-G_mc}, r_r = k e^{-b G_se}; error-correction by proofreading (Winfree & Bekbolatov, DNA9 2004) trades area for accuracy; Bennett 1979 "Dissipation-error tradeoff in proofreading" BioSystems 11, Hopfield 1974 PNAS 71, Ninio 1975: **kinetic proofreading** — fidelity bought with free energy. That is the physical precedent for the spine's "cost of settlement work".

## 4. Chemistry: AlChemy, RAF, organization

## 5. Causal side

## 6. Foam, sponge, vitrimer, bioelectric

### 6.1 Foam / von Neumann-Mullins
- J. von Neumann (1952), remarks in *Metal Interfaces*, ASM, Cleveland, 108-110; W. W. Mullins, "Two-dimensional motion of idealized grain boundaries", J. Appl. Phys. 27 (1956) 900-904 [R]. Statement: for a 2D cellular structure (dry foam, 120-degree trivalent vertices) with curvature-driven boundary motion, a cell with n sides changes area at constant rate dA/dt = K (n - 6), with K = (2 pi/3) M gamma for grain growth (M mobility, gamma energy) and K proportional to gas permeability times surface tension for bubbles; independent of cell shape or neighbours. n < 6 shrinks, n > 6 grows; <n> = 6 by Euler for trivalent planar graphs, so total area conserved. [R]
- 3D: R. D. MacPherson & D. J. Srolovitz, "The von Neumann relation generalized to coarsening of three-dimensional microstructures", Nature 446 (2007) 1053-1055 [R]: dV/dt = -2 pi M gamma (L(D) - (1/6) sum of edge lengths) ... where L is the mean width integral; **depends on geometry, not only topology**: no purely topological 3D law. So the spine's "coarsening ~ concepts losing support shrink and vanish" is a *topologically driven* law only in 2D.
- Plateau laws / Taylor: J. Taylor, "The structure of singularities in soap-bubble-like and soap-film-like minimal surfaces", Ann. Math. 103 (1976) 489-539 [R]: films meet 3 at 120 degrees, edges meet 4 at arccos(-1/3) ~ 109.47 degrees. Weaire & Phelan, Phil. Mag. Lett. 69 (1994) 107: A15-based partition beats Kelvin's truncated octahedron by ~0.3% in surface area. Weaire & Hutzler, *The Physics of Foams*, OUP 1999; Cantat et al., *Foams: Structure and Dynamics*, OUP 2013. Events: T1 (neighbour exchange; an edge shrinks to length zero then a new one grows in the perpendicular direction), T2 (disappearance of a 3-sided cell). Long-time: scaling state, mean area ~ t.
- Plug / HONESTY: the foam <-> concept lattice link is **analogy, no literature**. A foam's cell adjacency graph is a planar triangulation-dual, not a lattice; formal-concept bubbles share extents/intents (Galois), foam cells share walls (adjacency). Formal concept lattice order is containment, not adjacency. For the claim "bubbles are concepts" to have content define: adjacency = covering relation? Not planar in general (concept lattice Hasse diagrams are not planar). Keep foam as *dynamics/energy model on the adjacency graph of a clarified context* (Laguerre/power-diagram view, volumes as evidenced mass), where T1 = rewrite and T2 = vanishing support. The vN-M n-6 law then gives a **testable** prediction: cells with fewer than 6 neighbours lose evidential volume at a rate linear in (n - 6), if mjs implements curvature-driven (mean-curvature flow) dynamics; if it implements something else the law will not hold and the foam language should be dropped. The "volume constraint forbids trivial minimum" is correct: Plateau problem with enclosed volumes fixed.

### 6.3 Vitrimers
- D. Montarnal, M. Capelot, F. Tournilhac, L. Leibler, "Silica-like malleable materials from permanent organic networks", Science 334 (2011) 965-968 [R]. Epoxy-acid/anhydride networks with transesterification catalysts (e.g. Zn(OAc)2): covalent crosslinks exchange by an **associative** mechanism so the number of bonds (crosslink density) is constant at all temperatures; viscosity decreases with Arrhenius dependence, like strong glass-formers (silica); a "topology-freezing transition" T_v is conventionally where viscosity reaches ~10^12 Pa s; below T_v exchange is too slow to matter, above it the network flows and can be reprocessed. Where "new" would genuinely be (my judgement, bounded by what I found):
 1. A system whose *types are learned concepts of a distributional lattice* and whose derivations are checked by Lambek-sound residuation, with the **lattice computed exactly** rather than a vector approximation. Near relatives: Clark's CBFG/DLG (no neural, no negative lane), DisCoCat (vector semantics, hand-built types), Bradley-Terilla-Vlassopoulos (V-enriched, statistics-based).
 2. A **negative/FOUR evidence lane** inside the induction. Not found.
 3. **Ledgered energy accounting** (Zurek/Landauer + Hall EROI) as the fitness. Not found for grammar induction (found only for training-compute reporting).
 4. A cross-node, content-addressed, **registry-mediated heredity** of learned theories. Reminiscent of model hubs / federated learning but with immutable typed artifacts; no precedent found for typed theory packages.
 What would NOT be new: 100% on SCAN (several symbolic/neurosymbolic systems); near-perfect COGS structural splits (Lindemann et al.; Klinger et al.; RASP). A benchmark claim must go after: (i) data efficiency (CPG claims 14/22 examples), (ii) ReCOGS/SLOG/ gSCAN-style splits, (iii) learning from *unsegmented strings with no logical-form supervision* (Clark's regime) rather than parsing to supplied meaning, (iv) recovering the *correct* syntactic types (strong learning, Clark 2013). Proposed first falsifiable milestone: learn the SCL of a Dyck/regular/substitutable fragment (e.g. a SCAN-style grammar) from a few dozen strings and recover the Lambek lexicon, then check Wurm-sound derivations on held-out strings; compare sample complexity with CPG's 14/22.
