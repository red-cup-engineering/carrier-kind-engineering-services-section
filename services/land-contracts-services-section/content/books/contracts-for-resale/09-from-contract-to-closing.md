# From contract to closing

## Marketing the contract, never the land

The Group does not own the property it has under contract. It owns a right to
buy it. So the dispositions lead markets that right, the contract, and never
advertises the property as if the Group were selling it. Minnesota requires a
real estate license to list or advertise someone else's property for sale, and
the difference between "our contract to buy this parcel is available" and
"lot for sale" is the difference the law draws.

The contract goes first to the buyers whose buy box the parcel fits, on the
deal sheet (`contract-deal-sheet`): the parcel, its size and kind, the contract
price, the assignment price, the deposit, the closing date and the title
company, and one sentence saying plainly that the Group is offering its
contract right and does not own the land.

## The assignment

A buyer who wants it signs the assignment (`assignment-of-purchase-agreement`)
and pays a non-refundable deposit to the title company within two business
days. The assignment says the buyer takes all the Group's rights and duties
under the purchase agreement, pays the seller the contract price at closing,
and pays the Group the assignment fee. The deal is recorded as `assigned`.

## Closing

The title company closes on the date the purchase agreement sets. At closing:

- the buyer pays the contract price and the assignment fee;
- the title company pays the seller, less the seller's unpaid taxes and costs;
- the title company pays the Group the fee;
- the deed goes from the seller to the buyer.

The fee, and your share of it, are on the closing statement or paid within
five business days of the Group receiving the funds. The deal is recorded as
`closed`, and the fee counts in `land-activity`.

## When the Group buys outright

Sometimes the Group closes on the property itself and resells it: when a buyer
wants the property but cannot close inside the agreement's window, or when a
forfeited lot at county auction is cheap enough to hold. That takes the Group's
own money, so it is the exception, and your share on a resale is figured from
the resale profit after the purchase price, closing costs and holding costs.

## When a deal dies

Deals die, and that is part of the work. Whoever sees it die records it as
`dead` with the reason from its stage. The reasons add up in the funnel
(chapter 13), where the Group can see whether deals are being lost to price, to
title, to buyers, or to owners who stop answering, and change what it does.
