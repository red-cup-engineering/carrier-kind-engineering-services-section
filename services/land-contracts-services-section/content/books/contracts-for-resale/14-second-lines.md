# Second lines of work

## County auctions

Since July 2024, every Minnesota county must sell every tax-forfeited parcel
at a public auction, usually online, within six months, with at least 30 days'
published notice. The minimum bid is the unpaid taxes and costs. In St. Louis
County alone, thousands of parcels are forfeited land.

For the Group, the auction lists are supply. The acquisitions lead matches each
auction's parcels against the buy boxes, and tells matched buyers what is coming
up. When a parcel is cheap and a buyer wants it, the Group may bid and resell.
The lead list shows these parcels as kind `forfeited`, and you never call
anyone about them.

## Unclaimed surplus

When a forfeited parcel sells at auction for more than the taxes and costs,
the extra, the **surplus**, belongs to the former owner and anyone else with an
interest in the property. The county must mail them notice and a claim form,
send a second notice, and post the sale on its website. They have six months
from the first notice to claim; after that the county keeps the money.

Many former owners never claim, most often because they died and their heirs
do not know. Finding those heirs is the work of the Group's **heir search**
business. If you learn on a call that a property the family lost to
forfeiture sold at auction, tell the acquisitions lead: there may be money
waiting for the family, and the heir search business can help them find and
claim it.

## Estates

Hundreds of the parcels on the lists are held by estates, heirs or trusts. Some
are simple: a trustee or personal representative who can sign. Some are not: an
owner who died years ago, no estate ever opened, and a family who each own a
fraction without knowing it. Those parcels cannot be sold until someone
reconstructs the family's chain of title and a court or the family settles who
can sign.

That reconstruction is the heir search business's product, sold to the
family's attorney, the title company, or the nonprofits and programs that pay
for it. When you log `heir search` in a note, the acquisitions lead looks at
whether the family would be helped by it. When the title is cleared, the land
can come back to our pipeline, with the family choosing whether to sell.
