# Objections

An objection is information. The owner is telling you what matters to them.
Answer it truthfully, learn from it, and never argue.

| they say | you say |
|---|---|
| "How did you get my number?" | "Property ownership is public record at the county, and we look up owners of property we're interested in." |
| "It's worth more than that." (before any number) | "Could be. What do you think it's worth? I'll pass your number along exactly as you say it." |
| "Your letter's number is too low." | "That's why I'm glad you called. What number would work for you? I'll take it to our offer lead." |
| "I want to keep it for my kids." | "Understood. Is that still the plan, or has it been sitting? Either way, can I check back in six months?" Log `callback` with the date. |
| "Send me something in writing." | "Happy to. Our offer lead will call with a number and then send it in writing, with a letter that explains the agreement in plain words. What's the best email or address?" |
| "I'm not interested." | "No problem, thank you for your time. Would you like me to take you off our list?" Log `not interested` or `remove`. |
| "Are you a realtor?" | "No. We buy property directly. There's no agent and no commission." |
| "Why do you want it?" | For a lot: "Builders are looking for lots there, and yours could have a house on it." For land: "We buy land in [county] for people who want to hunt, build a cabin or hold it." For a house: "We buy rentals and houses that need work, and fix or rent them out." |
| "Are you going to flip it?" | "We might. We sign as the buyer 'or assigns', which means we may pass the contract to a builder or investor for a fee. That doesn't change your price or your closing, and we tell you so in writing before you sign." |
| "I'll list it with an agent." | "That's a good option, and you may get more. What we offer is a fast, certain sale with no commission or showings. If the listing doesn't work out, can I check back?" |
| "My brother owns half." | "Thanks, that helps. Would you both want to sell? Could I talk with him, or would you rather ask him first?" Every owner on title must sign. |
| "It's in my late mother's name." | "I'm sorry. Has anyone been appointed to handle her estate?" (chapter 5, estates) |
| "Stop calling me." | "I will. I'm taking you off our list now." Log `remove`. Never call that owner again. |

## The honest answer to "is this a scam?"

> I understand why you'd ask; people get a lot of these calls. We're the 561
> Group. We sign a written purchase agreement, the money goes through a
> Minnesota title company, and you can have a lawyer or anyone you trust read it
> before you sign. Nothing is paid by you, and nothing happens until you sign.

## When the owner seems confused

If the owner does not seem to understand what they own, what you are asking,
or what a sale would mean, stop selling. Say you would be glad to talk with a
family member or their lawyer instead, give your name and number, and log it
with a note. The Group does not buy from a person who cannot tell what they are
agreeing to. That is the one line the business will not cross.
