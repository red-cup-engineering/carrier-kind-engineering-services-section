# What we do

## The business in one paragraph

We find property whose owner would rather have a quick, certain sale than keep
it: a vacant lot inherited by someone three states away, forty acres of forest
nobody has walked in twenty years, a rental house a landlord is tired of. We
agree with the owner on a price and sign a purchase agreement that names us as
the buyer "or assigns". We then sell our right to buy, the contract, to a
builder or investor who wants the property, for a fee. Everyone closes at a
title company: the owner receives the agreed price, the new buyer receives the
property, and we receive the fee. We never have to own the property ourselves,
though we can when a deal calls for it.

The name for this is **contract assignment**. People in the trade call it
wholesaling. This book calls it what it is.

## Who pays whom

A deal has four parties and one closing.

| party | what they give | what they get |
|---|---|---|
| the owner (seller) | the property | the agreed price, paid at closing, with no commission and no showings |
| the buyer (assignee) | the price plus our fee | a property that fits what they build or hold, found and put under contract for them |
| the 561 Group | earnest money and the work of finding, contracting and closing | the assignment fee |
| you | the calls that find the seller or the buyer | your share of the fee, under your agreement |

The fee is the difference between what the buyer pays and what the owner
receives. It is paid by the buyer, through the title company, at closing. The
owner's price does not change because of it, and the owner is told in writing,
before signing, that we may assign the agreement for a fee (chapter 10).

## A deal, start to finish, with real numbers

This is the first lead on the Duluth list on 2026-09-26.

- **The property:** a half-acre of unimproved residential land in the Bay View
  Heights plat. At Duluth's standard lot of 25 by 125 feet, it holds about six
  building lots.
- **The owner:** lives in Florida. The county shows $1,579 in unpaid taxes
  and projects the lot will forfeit in 2026.
- **The county's estimate:** $14,400 for the land.
- **The buyer side:** a Duluth builder has told us they pay up to $12,000 for a
  buildable lot of this size in this part of town. That is the builder's **buy
  box** (chapter 3).

The numbers then run like this.

1. Our offer ceiling is the builder's price less the fee we need: $12,000
   less $3,000 is $9,000. The county-estimate ceiling (70% of $14,400,
   $10,100) is higher, so the builder's price sets the limit.
2. You reach the owner. They say they would take $9,000. You set an offer
   call.
3. The acquisitions lead offers $8,000 and settles there. The owner signs the
   purchase agreement and the disclosure letter. We deposit $300 of earnest
   money with a Duluth title company.
4. We send the builder the contract. They sign the assignment at $12,000 and
   pay a deposit.
5. At closing, the builder pays $12,000. The owner receives $8,000, less the
   $1,579 in back taxes the title company pays to the county. We receive
   $4,000.
6. At a 40% caller share, you receive $1,600 and the Group $2,400.

The owner got a sale they could not arrange from Florida, without an agent.
The builder got a lot without searching for it. You got paid for two phone
calls that mattered and the hundred that led to them.

## Why owners say yes

Owners do not sell to us because they are cornered. The 561 Group's own
research on this business says it plainly: sellers trade price for speed and
certainty all the time, for reasons that have nothing to do with hardship. An
heir who will never manage land a thousand miles away. A landlord ready to be
done with a tired rental. Someone relocating on a deadline. Someone who will not
pay for repairs or sit through showings. A fast, certain sale with no
commission is a real service, and it has a real price.

What makes a good lead is a **logistical** reason to sell: distance, time,
inheritance, a property the owner does not use. Chapter 4 shows how the lead
list finds those reasons in the county's records.

## Why buyers pay

Builders and investors pay the fee because finding property is expensive. A
builder who wants three lots in one neighborhood would have to read the
county's records, find the owners, reach them, and negotiate each one. We do
that across every parcel in the county at once, and bring them a contract that
already fits their price. The fee is the builder's search cost, paid only when
the search succeeds.

This is why the business works **buyers first**. We learn what buyers want
before we call a single owner, and we only call owners whose property a buyer
already wants.

## Who you work for

The 561 Group is a private company. It is the working structure of **Pheží
Ocháǧe**, a small Lakota kinship group (a tiyóšpaye) headed by the Group's
founder, emsenn. The Group exists to serve the kinship, not the other way
around. What the Group earns, including from this business, goes home to it.

You do not need to know more than that to do this work well. You should know
that it is true, because you may be asked, and because it is part of why the
Group insists on dealing honestly: the business is meant to last as long as the
people it serves.
