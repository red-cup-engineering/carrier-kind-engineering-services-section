# From yes to contract

Once you set an offer call, the acquisitions lead takes the deal forward. You
should know what happens next, because owners will ask you, and because your
share depends on it.

## The stages

Every deal moves through the same stages, and every stage has an office that
moves it on and a short list of reasons it can end there. Those reasons are
recorded when a deal dies, so the Group can see where deals are lost and fix
the procedure there.

| stage | means | who moves it on | it ends here when |
|---|---|---|---|
| lead | a parcel matched to a buyer, ready to call | you | wrong number, not the owner, owner died and no heir reached, never reached |
| contacted | the owner was reached | you | not interested, remove, keeping it, listed with an agent |
| interested | the owner named a price or asked for an offer | acquisitions lead | asking above the ceiling, no matched buyer, a title problem the owner named, owner stopped answering |
| offered | the Group made an offer | acquisitions lead | offer declined, owner stopped answering, another buyer |
| contracted | purchase agreement signed, earnest money deposited | dispositions lead | title failed, due diligence found a defect, seller backed out |
| marketed | the contract was sent to matched buyers | dispositions lead | no buyer at the assignment price, the contract expired |
| assigned | a buyer signed the assignment and paid its deposit | transaction coordinator | the buyer failed to close, title failed |
| closed | the title company closed and paid the fee | — | — |

## The offer

The acquisitions lead reads the lead and your notes, and calls the owner. The
offer comes from two numbers the list already has:

- the **opening offer**, near half the county's estimate for lots, sites and
  houses (less for rural land);
- the **ceiling**, the matched buyer's price less the Group's fee, never above
  70% of the county's estimate.

If the owner's asking price is under the ceiling, there is a deal. If it is
over, the lead may ask the buyer whether they would pay more, or thank the
owner and record the deal as dead with the reason `asking above ceiling`.
That reason is data: if many owners in a neighborhood ask above the ceiling,
the buyer's price there is too low, and the buy-box call should say so.

## The paperwork

The owner receives two documents, by e-signature or on paper:

1. **The disclosure letter** (`seller-disclosure-letter`). It says in plain
   words that the buyer is the Group "or assigns", that the Group may assign
   the agreement to a builder or investor for a fee paid by that buyer, that
   the owner's price does not change, that the Group is a buyer and not their
   agent, and that they may have anyone they trust read the agreement first.
2. **The purchase agreement** (`purchase-agreement`). Its sections, briefly:
   - the price, paid in cash at closing, with unpaid taxes paid from the
     seller's proceeds;
   - the earnest money, a few hundred dollars, held by the title company;
   - the closing date, 30 to 60 days out, at a named Minnesota title company;
   - who pays which costs (the buyer pays title and closing; the seller pays
     deed tax and fixes their own title problems);
   - a due-diligence window in which the buyer may inspect and walk away;
   - that the agreement may be assigned without the seller's consent, for a fee
     the assignee pays;
   - the residential disclosure, for a house, and the well and septic
     disclosure;
   - what happens if either side defaults.

For a Wisconsin parcel, a third document goes with them: the wholesaler notice
Wisconsin's Act 208 requires (chapter 10).

## Earnest money and title

Within three business days, the Group deposits the earnest money with the
title company named in the agreement, and the title company starts its title
search. The deal is recorded as `contracted` with the price, the earnest money,
the closing date, the title company, and the licensee representing the Group.

## What you tell the owner

If the owner calls you after signing, you can tell them:

- the title company will contact them about closing;
- they do not pay anything before closing;
- if they have questions about the agreement, the acquisitions lead or their
  own lawyer can answer them.

You do not change terms, and you do not promise anything the agreement does
not say.
