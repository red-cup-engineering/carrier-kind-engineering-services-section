# The numbers

## What a deal pays

| kind | a typical fee | why |
|---|---|---|
| a single infill lot | $2,000 to $10,000 | builders pay a few thousand over the contract price for a lot that fits |
| several lots or a site | more, often much more | the land is worth more and the builder's search cost is larger |
| rural or seasonal land | a few thousand to tens of thousands | offers open at a fifth to a half of value; buyers pay more |
| a house | several thousand to $20,000 or more | investors pay for houses that fit their rental or renovation plans |

Across the trade, a surveyed average assignment fee is about $13,000, with a
range from $2,500 to over $40,000; Duluth lots sit at the low end of that
range and houses and sites at the high end. Your agreement names your share;
the examples in this book use 40%.

## What calling yields

These figures come from firms that sell calling services, so the Group treats
them as ranges to test against your own numbers, not as promises.

- On a good list, about one dial in ten reaches a person.
- About one in twelve of those conversations becomes a qualified lead: an
  owner with a number and a reason.
- Three to eight percent of qualified leads sign.
- Put together: one signed contract for every 800 to 1,500 dials.

Lists matched to a buyer's buy box, which is what your call queue gives you,
are the good-list case. Old or unmatched lists take several times as many dials.

## What that means for you

At 100 dials a day, a contract comes every 8 to 15 working days: one and a half
to two and a half a month. At a $4,000 fee and a 40% share, that is $1,600 a
deal, or about $2,400 to $4,000 a month. Letters add to that without adding
dials. Houses and sites raise the fee per deal.

These are arithmetic from the trade's averages, not a promise. Your own numbers,
in `land-activity`, are the only ones that count, and after a month you will
have enough of them to plan with.

## How your share is recorded and paid

When a deal closes, the closing is recorded with the fee the title company paid, and your share
is written into it: the deal is yours if you were the first to log the owner as interested (or
set the offer call) before the Group made its offer. The share is the enterprise's caller-share
setting at the time, 40% unless the acquisitions lead has recorded a different point and why.
The acquisitions lead pays what closings owe you and records each payment, and the Group's books
show your share as owed from the closing until it is paid.

## Reading your funnel

The funnel (`land-activity`, `land-pipeline`) shows how many deals reached each
stage and the share of each stage that went on to the next. Read it from the
top.

- **Few conversations per dial:** the numbers are bad or the times are wrong.
- **Few interested per conversation:** the list or the opening is wrong.
- **Many interested, few offered:** asking prices are above the ceiling, or
  there is no matched buyer. Call buyers.
- **Many offered, few contracted:** the offers are too low for these owners, or
  the offer call comes too late. Tell the acquisitions lead.
- **Contracted but not assigned:** the buyers' boxes are stale.

The reasons deals died, by stage, are listed under the funnel. They are the
most useful numbers the Group has.
