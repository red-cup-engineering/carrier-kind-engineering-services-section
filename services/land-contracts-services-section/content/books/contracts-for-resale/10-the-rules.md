# The rules

These are the rules you work under. Some are Minnesota's and Wisconsin's law.
Some are the Group's. Each comes with its reason.

## You are a partner in the deal, not an agent

Minnesota law defines a real estate broker as anyone who, **for another** and
for a fee, buys, sells, or negotiates the purchase or sale of real estate, or
advertises that they do (Minnesota Statutes 82.55, subdivision 19). Doing that
without a license is not allowed.

That is why you are paid as a joint-venture partner in each deal you bring in,
under the caller agreement, and not as a commissioned agent negotiating
someone else's sale. It is also why the work is divided the way it is:

- **You** find and qualify owners and buyers, learn what they want, and set
  offer calls.
- **The Group** makes every offer, signs every agreement, holds every deposit,
  and markets its contracts.

So: never quote a firm price, never agree to terms, never sign for the Group,
and never describe yourself as representing the owner or the buyer.

## The Group's own licensing line

The same statute presumes that anyone who takes part as principal in **five
or more** real estate transactions in twelve months is in the business of
selling real estate, unless a licensed broker or salesperson represents them
(subdivision 19, clause g). The Group intends to do far more than five a year,
so every contract it signs names the licensed broker or salesperson
representing it. The pipeline counts the contracts in the last twelve months so
the acquisitions lead always knows where the Group stands. Builders are exempt
up to 25 transactions a year (Minnesota Statutes 82.56); the Group, which does
not build, is not.

If you hold a Minnesota real estate license yourself, tell the acquisitions
lead: the rules for licensees are different, and so is how you can be paid.

## The owner always knows we may assign

Every owner receives the disclosure letter before signing, and the purchase
agreement says the Group may assign it for a fee. An owner who does not know
they are selling to someone who may assign has not agreed to it.

## Wisconsin

Wisconsin (Superior, across the bridge from Duluth) calls someone who buys
under a purchase agreement intending to assign it a **real property
wholesaler**, and requires written notice of that to the seller no later than
signing, and to the buyer who takes the assignment (2023 Wisconsin Act 208). A
seller who did not get the notice may cancel before closing and keep the
deposit. Every Wisconsin deal carries the notice.

## Calling

- **Dial by hand, one call at a time, as a person.** Minnesota forbids
  machines that dial and play a recorded message unless the person agreed to
  it or a live person comes on first, and never before 9 a.m. or after 9 p.m.
  (Minnesota Statutes 325E.27). We do not use them at all.
- **Call between 8 a.m. and 9 p.m. in the owner's time zone.** Check where
  they live before you dial.
- **Say who you are and why you are calling, truthfully.**
- **When someone asks to be taken off the list, take them off**, log
  `remove`, and never call them again.
- **Offers to buy are not telemarketing.** Federal courts have mostly held
  that a call asking whether an owner will sell, and offering to buy, is not
  a "telephone solicitation" under the federal telephone law, because that law
  is about selling things to the person called. That holds only while the call
  is what it says it is: a genuine offer to buy their property. Never use a
  seller call to sell the owner anything, and never gather owners' information
  to pass to anyone else.

## How we treat people

The Group's research into this business found a documented pattern in the
wider trade: pressing unsolicited low offers on people in distress, especially
elderly homeowners and Black and Brown neighborhoods, which is the reason
several cities and states have passed homeowner-protection laws. The Group's
answer is to be a business that looks nothing like that, which is also how you
earn trust fast on a cold call. The lines:

1. **No fraud.** Say only what is true, about us, about the process, about the
   county's records, and about the law.
2. **No pressure on a person who cannot tell what they are agreeing to.** If
   an owner seems confused about what they own or what a sale means, stop, offer
   to speak with family or a lawyer instead, and log it (chapter 6).
3. **Prefer the logistical seller.** The best leads are owners who live far
   away, inherited something they do not use, or are done being landlords.
   Those are the leads the list scores highest, and they are the ones who
   benefit most from a fast, certain sale.
4. **Tell them they can go elsewhere.** An owner can always list with an
   agent, or have a lawyer read our agreement. Say so when it comes up. It costs
   nothing and it is true.

## Records

Everything you do is logged in the Group's records, under your account: every
dial, letter, buy box and note. That is how you are paid, how deals are traced,
and how the procedure improves. Keep your notes factual; owners can ask what
we have recorded about them, and anything you write should be something you
would be comfortable reading to them.
