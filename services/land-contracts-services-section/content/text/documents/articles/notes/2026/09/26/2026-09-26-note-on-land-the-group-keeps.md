# Land the Group keeps

## Why the business keeps land

The [land contracts business plan](2026-09-26-note-on-the-land-contracts-business-plan.md)
earns fees by assigning contracts on land it never owns. This note is the
other half: a share of those fees buys land the Group keeps.

The founder's writing already asks for this, well before the business
existed. The
[2024-12-27 planning statement](../../../../../../../../../../../../../561-group-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2024/12/27/2024-12-27-planning-statement.md)
sets as goals that "Land funds itself," "land is controlled by LLC," and
"Income covers land stewardship," with birch sap, mycoculture, vermicompost,
foraging and a local market as the land's work, and "Keep land owned by 1
person" so that someone else in the household stays a first-time homebuyer.
The 2023 letter
[Fall Arrives](../../../../../../../../../../../personal-services-section/services/emsenn-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/essays/fall-arrives-2023.md)
asks for "a couple acres of secure land access" on which to practice. The
[Pheží Ocháǧe note](../../../../../../../../../../../../../561-group-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/21/2026-08-21-note-on-phezi-ochage.md)
says the band and its land keep each other: "the land needs the band and the
band the land."
The business now has the income to act on those goals.

The law and numbers are in the
[note on Minnesota law for acquiring and holding land](../../../../../../../../../../../../../science-services-section/services/social-science-services-section/services/law-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/26/2026-09-26-note-on-minnesota-law-for-acquiring-and-holding-land.md)
and the
[note on free lots and holding land in northeastern Minnesota](../../../../../../../../../../../../../science-services-section/services/social-science-services-section/services/economics-and-business-science-services-section/services/economics-science-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/09/26/2026-09-26-note-on-free-lots-and-holding-land-in-northeastern-minnesota.md).

## What land

Forest and rural acreage of **20 acres or more**, because 20 contiguous
forested acres is the threshold for the Sustainable Forest Incentive Act and
for the managed-forest tax class, and so the size at which land pays its own
tax. The first market is St. Louis County's towns and unorganized land, where
rural vacant land carries a county estimate of about $1,060 an acre (median)
and 4,281 parcels of ten acres or more are estimated at $25,000 or less. Lake,
Carlton, Itasca and Aitkin counties are next, from the statewide layer and
their own sale lists. Within a drive of Duluth comes first, because the land
is to be worked.

## How the Group buys it

On 2026-09-27 St. Louis County's lists held 14 unsold tracts of 20 acres or
more in its towns that it will sell on terms, at about $450 to $650 an acre.
Among them, over the counter now: 40 acres in the Town of Cotton at $19,200
($1,920 down) and 40 acres in Great Scott at $20,060. At the November land
sale auction: 37 acres in Pike at $16,900 and 20 acres in Kugler with about
2,000 feet of river frontage at $12,200. That is roughly half the county's
median rural estimate for private land.

1. **County lists, on terms.** St. Louis County sells forfeited land online
   and then over the counter, first come, first served, at the listed price.
   Standard sales can be bought on terms of 10% or $500 down, up to ten years,
   at 7%. This is the cheapest money the Group can get, and it needs no bank.
   The county holds the deed until the last payment; timber cannot be cut
   until its appraised value is paid; a missed installment or a missed year's
   taxes lets the county cancel. The transaction coordinator reads each
   county's Available List and auction list against the Group's own buy box.
2. **Owners the call lists already find.** The land business's call queue
   reads rural acreage and its owners (out of state, behind on taxes, estates).
   When no outside buyer's box fits a parcel and the Group's does, the Group is
   the buyer: the acquisitions lead offers in the blind-offer range (a quarter
   to under half of the county's estimate) and the Group closes in its own
   name. The Group records its own buy box with `land-buy-box-record` like any
   other buyer, so its matches come through the same queue.
3. **Auctions.** Every forfeited parcel is auctioned within six months of
   forfeiture; the Group bids where the minimum bid is under its box's price.

Buying for the Group's own account is a transaction as principal. It counts
toward the five-in-twelve-months presumption of Minn. Stat. 82.55, subd. 19(g)
like an assignment does, and the deal record names the licensee representing
the Group (the rule `land-contracts-name-their-representation`).

## How the land pays for itself

A worked example: 40 acres at the county median ($42,400), bought on county
terms, costs $4,240 down plus about $1,480 in fees. The first year's
installment is about $6,487, and property tax runs about $200–$400 a year.

- **The forest incentive.** Once a DNR plan is written ($300 + $10 an acre,
  50–75% cost-shared), an 8-year SFIA covenant pays $12.13 an acre, $485 a
  year on 40 acres, more than the tax. A 20-year covenant pays $16.79 and a
  50-year one $21.46. The covenant bars development, so a parcel meant for a
  house is left out of it or kept to the managed-forest class instead.
- **Timber** at stumpage (aspen went for $30.73 a cord in 2021) when the plan
  calls for a harvest.
- **Sap and forest products**: the birch sap, syrup, mushrooms and forage of
  the 2024 plan, sold into the local market that plan names.
- **Leases** to hunters, and carbon payments where the stand is hardwood
  enough to qualify (30 acres or more under the Family Forest Carbon Program).
- **Resale on terms.** Some parcels are bought to resell to recreational
  buyers on a contract for deed at 10–20% down. The payments carry the parcels
  kept. Vacant land is outside chapter 559A; the contract is recorded within
  four months and cancelled on 60 days' notice (90 for an investor seller).

## Money in, as a rule

A share of every net assignment fee goes to a land fund that pays down
payments, installments and taxes, and nothing else. At the plan's example deal
the Group keeps $2,400 of a $4,000 fee. Half of that into the fund pays the
$500 down payment on a small over-the-counter parcel, or 10% down on a
$12,000 one, from the first assignment. Two assignments a month from the
second quarter put roughly $2,400 a month into the fund: a median 40-acre
parcel's down payment and fees (about $5,700) every two to three months,
with the installments and taxes of the parcels already held paid first.

The share is the land enterprise's `land-fund-share` setting (half by
default), beside its offer and fee settings, so the fund's movements are
records in the same journal as the deals that fed it.

## Title

The planning statement's rule holds: land sits with one person or one company
(an LLC costs $155 online and has no annual fee). Owning vacant or rural land
does not end anyone's first-time-homebuyer standing, which turns only on
owning a principal residence, so the member who will buy the household's
house keeps that standing whoever holds the land (law note, section 6).

## First moves

1. Form the land-holding LLC, or settle which member holds title directly.
2. Record the Group's buy box: St. Louis, Lake, Carlton counties; rural
   acreage and forfeited land; 20 acres or more; top price per acre; terms
   accepted.
3. Pull St. Louis County's lists (`land-sale-lists-pull`) and read the tracts
   that fit the box (`land-sale-lists buyer="561 Group"`).
4. From the first assignment fee, put the down payment on the best of them.
5. Order the DNR stewardship plan and enroll in SFIA the year after purchase.

## Where it runs

- `land-sale-lists-pull county=st-louis` reads every list St. Louis County's
  sales page links (auctions and the over-the-counter available lists) and
  `land-sale-lists` shows the unsold tracts with the buy boxes each fits and
  the down payment and first installment on the county's terms. The same
  lists serve the business's auction line in
  [chapter 14 of the training book](../../../../../../../books/contracts-for-resale/14-second-lines.md).
  Lake, Itasca, Aitkin and Carlton counties publish their lists differently
  and are added one at a time.
- Each closing reserves `land-fund-share` (half by default) of the Group's net
  fee to the land fund. `land-holding-record` records a purchase, an
  installment, a tax bill or an incentive payment; `land-holdings` reads the
  parcels held, the installments coming due and the fund's balance; and
  `ledger-settlement-proposal` posts it all (the fund as
  `Assets:Cash:Reserved:Land`, the land as `Assets:Land:<parcel>`, the
  county's balance as `Liabilities:Land-Contracts:<parcel>`).
